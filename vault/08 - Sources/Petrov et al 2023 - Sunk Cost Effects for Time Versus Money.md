# Petrov et al 2023 - Sunk Cost Effects for Time Versus Money

*International Review of Social Psychology*, 28 November 2023. A Registered Report: close,
high-powered replication and extension of Soman (2001).

## What it supports

Two things at once: that the core effect **reappears** under preregistration, and that the popular
claim "sunk time matters less than sunk money" **does not hold up cleanly**.

## Key claims

- N = 821, US MTurk sample. Registered Report, so the analyses were fixed before data collection.
- **Study 1** (theatre/concert tickets): original ϕc = .61 [.43, .78]; replication ϕc = .38
  [.31, .45]. Same direction — money effects stronger than time — but **markedly smaller**.
- **Study 2** (rocket engine project): original found money ϕc = .32 and *no* time effect (ϕc = .02).
  The replication found **both**: money ϕc = .23, time ϕc = .32.
- **Study 5** (education/opportunity costs): the replication **failed to find support for any of the
  effects**.
- Overall: mixed support. Sunk time effects are real in at least some designs.

## Caveats

- A single online US sample; MTurk populations are not representative.
- Directly relevant to Soman's time-vs-money hypothesis, not to whether sunk cost exists at all.
- Illustrates ordinary effect-size shrinkage in replication as much as any specific theoretical
  failure.
