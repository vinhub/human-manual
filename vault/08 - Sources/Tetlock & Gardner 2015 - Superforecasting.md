# Tetlock & Gardner 2015 - Superforecasting

Popular account of the Good Judgment Project, the forecasting tournament run for IARPA.

## What it supports

That forecasting accuracy is **measurable and trainable** — the basis for treating decision-making as
a skill with feedback rather than a fixed trait.

## Key claims

- Forecasts scored with Brier scores, making accuracy comparable across people and questions.
- A subset of ordinary volunteers ("superforecasters"), identified from tournament data and trained
  in probabilistic reasoning and reference-class forecasting, substantially outperformed the field
  and reportedly beat intelligence-community baselines.
- Roughly 70% retained superforecaster status year to year, arguing against pure luck.
- Reference-class forecasting is singled out as among the most valuable trainable components.

## Caveats

- **This is a level-6 source on the [[Source Evaluation]] hierarchy — a popular summary.** It is
  cited here as a placeholder for the underlying literature and should be **replaced by the primary
  papers** (the Good Judgment Project publications by Mellers, Tetlock and colleagues) before any
  quantitative claim from it is published.
- The specific figures circulating for the margin over baselines come from secondary retellings and
  have not been checked against the primary reports.
- Tournament questions are resolvable geopolitical forecasts over months. Generalising to
  long-horizon or unresolvable decisions is unwarranted.
