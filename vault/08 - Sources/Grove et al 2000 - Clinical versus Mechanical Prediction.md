# Grove et al 2000 - Clinical versus Mechanical Prediction

*Psychological Assessment*, 12(1), 19–30. A meta-analysis of clinical versus mechanical prediction.

## What it supports

That simple formal rules generally match or beat expert intuitive judgement in predicting human
health and behaviour — the empirical backbone for preferring an explicit decision procedure over
holistic expert impression.

## Key claims

- Mechanical (formal, statistical) prediction was on average **about 10% more accurate** than
  clinical prediction.
- Mechanical prediction **substantially outperformed** clinical judgement in **33–47%** of studies,
  depending on the analysis.
- Clinical judgement was substantially more accurate in only **6–16%** of studies.
- The advantage held **regardless of judgement task, type of judge, judge experience, or data type**.

## Caveats

- "About 10% more accurate" is an average across heterogeneous domains; it is not a promise about any
  particular decision.
- Clinical judgement was often *equal*, not routinely disastrous. The claim is a consistent modest
  edge, not expert incompetence.
- Domain is human health and behaviour. Extending it to all decision-making is an extrapolation.
- Mechanical rules require a decent outcome measure and a dataset. Where neither exists, this result
  does not tell you what to do.
