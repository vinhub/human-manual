# Białek & Biesiada 2025 - On the Low Reliability of Sunk Cost Vignettes

*Brain Sciences*, 15(8), 808. Published 28 July 2025.

## What it supports

The **measurement critique**. This is the most important caveat attached to the sunk cost literature
and the reason [[Sunk Cost Fallacy]] marks its individual-difference claims as contested.

## Key claims

- Sunk cost bias is among the most studied decision-making biases, yet decades of vignette-based
  research have **never been systematically psychometrically validated**.
- Testing whether the widely-used scenarios measure the same underlying construct: **internal
  consistency was consistently poor, ω = 0.14–0.57**, with weak intercorrelations between scenarios.
- Implication: responses to sunk cost vignettes are substantially scenario-specific. There may be no
  stable "sunk cost susceptibility" trait that these instruments capture.

## Caveats

- A psychometric result about *instruments*, not a refutation of the *effect*. Aggregate,
  within-subject experimental demonstrations (e.g.
  [[Arkes & Blumer 1985 - The Psychology of Sunk Cost]],
  [[Ronayne Sgroi & Tuckwell 2021 - Evaluating the Sunk Cost Effect]]) are not undermined by low
  scale reliability.
- Recent (2025) and not yet widely replicated or absorbed by the field.
- *Brain Sciences* (MDPI) is a lower-prestige venue than the other sources here; weight accordingly
  until corroborated.
