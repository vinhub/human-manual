# Arkes & Blumer 1985 - The Psychology of Sunk Cost

*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 35(1), 124–140.

## What it supports

The foundational demonstration that irrecoverable prior costs change subsequent behaviour, in both
field and laboratory settings. This is the paper that named the effect and set the research agenda.

## Key claims

- **Field experiment (Ohio University theatre).** Season-ticket buyers were randomly assigned to pay
  full price ($15), a $2 discount, or a $7 discount. Those who paid full price attended more plays in
  the first half of the season (≈4.11 vs ≈3.3). Attendance decreased as the discount increased.
- Because the discount was randomly assigned, the groups did not differ in enthusiasm for theatre.
  The only thing that varied was money already spent and unrecoverable.
- Across a series of scenario experiments, the same pattern appears: prior investment increases the
  tendency to continue.
- The authors' proposed mechanism is **the desire not to appear wasteful** — an overgeneralised
  social norm rather than a primitive cognitive limitation.

## Caveats

- The field study is small and from a single institution in 1985; the attendance difference appears
  in the first half of the season and attenuates later.
- Most of the supporting experiments are hypothetical scenarios, which later work
  ([[Białek & Biesiada 2025 - On the Low Reliability of Sunk Cost Vignettes]]) shows are
  psychometrically weak.
- The waste-avoidance mechanism is the authors' interpretation, not something the design isolates.
  Competing mechanisms explain the same data (see
  [[Sleesman et al 2012 - Cleaning Up the Big Muddy]]).
