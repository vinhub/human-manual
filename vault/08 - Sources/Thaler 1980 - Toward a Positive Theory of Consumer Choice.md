# Thaler 1980 - Toward a Positive Theory of Consumer Choice

*Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 1(1), 39–60.

## What it supports

The theoretical framing that places sunk cost within **mental accounting** and prospect theory,
rather than treating it as an isolated quirk.

## Key claims

- Consumers systematically violate standard economic theory in well-defined ways, so the theory makes
  *predictable* errors — not random noise.
- Prospect theory is proposed as the descriptive alternative.
- Among the catalogued deviations: underweighting of opportunity costs, **failure to ignore sunk
  costs**, search behaviour, choosing not to choose and regret, and precommitment/self-control.
- Framing an abandoned project as closing a mental account at a loss explains why continuing feels
  cheaper than stopping, even when it is not.

## Caveats

- This is theory and synthesis, not new experimental evidence. It should be cited for the
  *explanatory frame*, never as evidence that the effect exists.
- Mental accounting is one of several live mechanisms; the paper's influence should not be mistaken
  for the mechanism being settled.
