# Ronayne Sgroi & Tuckwell 2021 - Evaluating the Sunk Cost Effect

*Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 186, 318–327.

## What it supports

That the effect survives **incentivised, non-hypothetical** conditions — the strongest answer to the
objection that sunk cost is an artefact of asking people about imaginary theatre tickets.

## Key claims

- Subjects earned a lottery through a real-effort task, then were offered the chance to switch to a
  **dominant** lottery. **23% stuck with the dominated one.** Sticking was strictly worse by
  construction.
- The **endowment effect accounts for roughly one third** of this, so most of it is not mere
  attachment to the object.
- **Cognitive reflection** is a significant predictor of susceptibility.
- Crystallised intelligence (stocks of knowledge/experience) predicts sunk cost behaviour, whereas
  fluid/algorithmic thinking and openness do not.
- The authors construct and validate a measurement scale, **SCE-8**, spanning multiple resource
  types.

## Caveats

- A single experimental paradigm; the lottery-switch task is cleaner than real life but narrower.
- The individual-difference findings sit uneasily beside
  [[Białek & Biesiada 2025 - On the Low Reliability of Sunk Cost Vignettes]], which questions whether
  vignette-based sunk cost measures cohere at all. SCE-8 was built partly in response to that class
  of problem, but the tension is unresolved.
- "23% stuck" is a rate in one design, not a general population base rate.
