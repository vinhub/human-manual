# Sleesman et al 2012 - Cleaning Up the Big Muddy

*Academy of Management Journal*, 55(3), 541–562. A meta-analytic review of the determinants of
escalation of commitment.

## What it supports

The organisational-scale cousin of sunk cost — **escalation of commitment** — and, more usefully, a
comparison of how well the competing *theoretical* explanations actually perform.

## Key claims

- Escalation of commitment has been studied for 35+ years with many proposed determinants; this
  aggregates them.
- It evaluates the relative explanatory power of the competing theoretical perspectives rather than
  confirming one.
- Escalation is driven by a mix of project, psychological, social, and structural determinants —
  meaning it is **not** purely an individual reasoning error. Incentives and accountability
  structures matter.

## Caveats

- Escalation of commitment and the sunk cost fallacy overlap but are not identical: escalation
  concerns continued *investment* in a failing course of action, often over time and inside
  organisations, and can be driven by incentives that make continuing individually rational.
- Heavily weighted toward organisational and managerial samples.
- I have not read the full text; the specific effect sizes and the ranking of theories should be
  checked before any are quoted.
